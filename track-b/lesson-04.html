Monte Carlo — the method that trades certainty for dimensions, at 1/√N

When integrals live in 100 dimensions (finance, particle transport, statistical physics), grids die exponentially and random sampling doesn't care. The price is the slowest convergence law in this course — error ∝ 1/√N, measured below at slope −0.494 — which makes Monte Carlo simultaneously the most GPU-hungry workload in science and the field where ALGEBRA (variance reduction, measured at 62×) beats hardware hardest. Plus the parallel-randomness problem cuRAND solved properly, and folklore didn't.

Concept

The law: 1/√N, measured — including a trap about measuring it

estimate π: throw darts at the unit square, count the quarter-circle hits
  error ~ σ/√N   (CLT: the estimator is a mean of N iid samples)
MEASURED, RMS over 30 trials:  N=1e3 → 4.9e-2 · 1e4 → 1.4e-2 · 1e5 → 5.2e-3
  · 1e6 → 1.5e-3     log-log slope = −0.494  ✓ theory −0.5
THE TRAP (executed on purpose): a SINGLE run's error-vs-N slope read −0.80 —
  one trajectory's errors are themselves random; fitting them "measures" a
  rate that isn't there. Convergence rates get trials and error bars like
  any other measurement (M1 L7, pointed at the method itself).
the trade: ×100 samples → ÷10 error — brutal, but DIMENSION-FREE: a grid
  with 100 points/axis in d=10 needs 10²⁰ evaluations; MC needs whatever
  your error budget says, in ANY d. That exchange built quant finance.

Parallel randomness: independence by construction

the catastrophe (measured): rng(123) on every rank → correlation 1.0 —
  a million "samples" carrying ONE sample's information; error bars lie 1000×
the folklore fix (seed+rank): usually-okay with modern generators, PROVABLY
  okay with none — and historic LCG/MT crews got burned enough that
  "usually" is banned in this track (B8's reproducibility ethics)
the correct constructions:
  numpy: SeedSequence(seed).spawn(n_ranks) — measured cross-corr −0.02 ✓
  cuRAND device API: counter-based Philox — sample = f(key, counter):
    stream (seed, subsequence, offset) gives every GPU THREAD its own
    provably-disjoint sequence, reproducible regardless of thread count —
    5.8's determinism problem, solved at the SOURCE for randomness
    (curand_init(seed, tid, 0, &state) — one line, mathematically clean)

Variance reduction: algebra as a sample multiplier

antithetic: average f(U) with f(1−U) — errors anti-correlate when f is
  MONOTONE.  MEASURED: var 0.242 → 0.0039 = 62× fewer samples free.
  (non-monotone f? the pairing can do nothing — or harm: the WHY is load-
  bearing, quiz 2's whole case)
control variates: know E[g] exactly for a g correlated with f? estimate
  E[f−c·g] + c·E[g] — variance shrinks by the correlation²
stratified / quasi-MC (Sobol): spread samples evenly → up to ~1/N on smooth
  integrands (a whole order of convergence!) at the cost of trickier error
  estimation — the standard finance stack is Sobol + Brownian bridges
the doctrine: variance reduction is measured in ×fewer-samples, making it
  directly comparable to buying GPUs — 62× from four lines of algebra is
  a fleet you didn't purchase (4.6's economics, probabilistic edition).

Why GPUs eat Monte Carlo alive

N paths, zero coupling until the final average: embarrassingly parallel —
  5.2's independent-threads model with no halos, no barriers (until the
  reduction: 5.8, verbatim), no divergence IF paths take similar branches
per-thread shape: private state (Philox counters in registers), a path loop,
  one value out → grid-stride + 5.8's ladder for the mean/variance
the ONE wrinkle: payoffs with branchy logic (barrier options, absorption
  events) reintroduce 5.6's divergence — sort/bucket paths by fate when it
  bites (5.6 Ex.1's remedy, probabilistic edition).

Worked example

Task: Price the classic pipeline — estimate E[e^U] (a baby option payoff) to ±1e-4 at 95% confidence, three ways, with the sample-count arithmetic done BEFORE any run (the B-track ritual). All variances measured above.

required N ≈ (1.96·σ / ε)²   for ±ε at 95%
plain MC:        σ² = 0.242  → N = (1.96·0.492/1e-4)² ≈ 9.3e7 samples
antithetic:      σ² = 0.0039 → N ≈ 1.5e6   (62× fewer — the measured ratio,
                                            cashing out exactly as predicted)
+ control variate (g=U, E[U]=½, corr(e^U,U)≈0.99): variance ÷ ~50 again →
                 N ≈ 3e4 — from 93 MILLION to 30 THOUSAND samples by algebra
GPU ledger (T4-class): plain-MC 9.3e7 paths × ~20 ns/path ≈ 1.9 s;
  the algebraic stack does it in ~1 ms — OR holds the 1.9 s budget and
  delivers ±2e-6 instead. Either way: the variance table IS the capacity plan.
error-bar fixture (non-negotiable): report estimate ± 1.96·s/√N from the
  SAME run's sample variance, and CI-test that the true value lands inside
  the bar ~95% of the time across seeds — an estimator whose error bars
  don't calibrate is wrong even when its point estimate is close (B8's
  statistical-reproducibility rung, born here).

What to internalize: the entire engineering conversation — how many samples, how much GPU, whether to bother with variance reduction — happened in four lines of arithmetic on MEASURED variances. Monte Carlo without the N-formula is gambling with error bars; with it, it's the most predictable workload in this track: embarrassingly parallel, bandwidth-light, and priced to the sample before you launch anything.

Key points

  • MC error ∝ σ/√N (measured slope −0.494 over 30-trial RMS) — dimension-free, which is the entire reason it owns high-dimensional integration despite the brutal rate.
  • Measuring a convergence rate is itself statistics: a single run's slope read −0.80; rates get trials, RMS, and error bars (M1 L7 applied recursively).
  • Parallel streams are independent by CONSTRUCTION or not at all: same-seed corr = 1.0 (measured); SeedSequence.spawn / counter-based Philox (cuRAND's device API) make disjointness a theorem and reproducibility thread-count-independent.
  • Variance reduction is a sample multiplier you can price: antithetic measured at 62× on monotone f; control variates scale with corr²; Sobol approaches 1/N on smooth integrands — algebra competes with fleets.
  • Required N = (1.96σ/ε)² — the four-line capacity plan that converts a measured variance into GPUs, seconds, and error bars before any run.
  • Error bars are part of the estimator: report ±1.96s/√N from the run itself, and fixture their CALIBRATION across seeds — a close point estimate with lying bars is still wrong.

Quiz

1. A risk team scales their GPU Monte Carlo from 8 to 512 workers for a regulatory deadline. Throughput scales beautifully; the final VaR number's error bar, computed as always from sample variance, SHRINKS on schedule — but backtesting later shows the estimate was far outside its own confidence interval, badly. The RNG line: curand_init(42 + worker_id % 16, 0, 0, &state). Reconstruct the failure, explain why the error bar LIED, and give the two-line fix plus the fixture that catches this class forever.
The % 16 is a real-world bug shape (a modulo meant for something else, a copy-paste from a 16-GPU config) — and its cruelty is that MORE hardware made the statistics WORSE while every dashboard improved. The mechanism to internalize: duplicated streams shrink effective N invisibly to variance-based bars, because the bar formula ASSUMES independence — the one assumption the bug broke. Calibration fixtures test the assumption itself, which is why B8 makes them mandatory for anything reported with a ±.
2. Fresh from this lesson, a colleague applies antithetic pairing to their photon-transport code (payoff: 1 if the photon escapes a slab, 0 if absorbed — a jagged, non-monotone function of the driving uniforms) and reports "variance reduced 1.7×… sometimes, and 0.95× on other configs." Then they propose Sobol sequences "for the 1/N rate" on the same 60-dimensional discontinuous problem. Set expectations with mechanisms, and redirect to the two techniques that DO fit this problem.
The 62× was real AND conditional — this quiz is the price of teaching by measurement: learners over-generalize the number and skip the hypothesis (monotonicity). The redirect matters as much as the rejection: importance sampling is where transport codes live (and its likelihood-ratio machinery is exactly what B8 will audit for weight-degeneracy). "Technique X reduced variance 62×" is always shorthand for "…on integrands satisfying its hypotheses" — reading the fine print is the difference between a method and a slogan.

Go deeper — the stochastic craft sheet

1. From uniforms to everything (the transformation toolbox)

inverse CDF: x = F⁻¹(u) — exact, one uniform per sample, needs invertible F
  (exponential: −ln(u)/λ — transport's free-path draw, one line)
Box–Muller: two uniforms → two exact Gaussians (cuRAND's normal2 does pairs —
  half the calls, and the paired shape fits 5.7's vectorized loads)
acceptance–rejection: propose, test, retry — exact for gnarly densities but
  BRANCHY: the GPU wrinkle (§4) made systematic; batch-and-compact
  (Thrust's B9 stream compaction) beats per-thread retry loops
ziggurat: table-based Gaussian/exponential — fastest on CPU, table lookups
  = gathers on GPU (5.7): cuRAND mostly prefers Box–Muller/Philox math.
choose by: exactness need × branch cost × memory pattern — a distributions
decision is a kernel-shape decision (every table in this track ends there).

2. Error bars in production (the part beyond ±1.96σ)

batch means: correlated samples (MCMC, time-series paths) violate iid —
  bar formulas need effective sample size (autocorrelation-adjusted) or
  batched variances: the ±'s hypotheses, again
weight degeneracy (importance sampling's tax): if a few likelihood ratios
  dominate, effective N collapses — monitor ESS = (Σw)²/Σw²; ESS/N < 10%
  means your clever sampler is a lottery (quiz 2's redirect, audited)
seeds in CI: fixture runs pin seeds for bitwise repro (b8-repro rung 1);
  VALIDATION runs sweep seeds for calibration (rung 3) — two jobs, two
  configs, never confused (the B8 ladder operationalized for MC).

3. Gotchas

Seed arithmetic. Any formula fancier than "distinct subsequence per worker" (quiz 1's %16, seed+rank×constant, time-based seeds colliding at launch) is a correlation bug waiting for scale. Philox subsequences or SeedSequence.spawn — constructions, not arithmetic.
One RNG state in shared/global memory. Threads sharing a generator state serialize on it (5.8's contention) or race it (corrupted streams — worse than slow). State lives per-thread in registers; that's WHY counter-based designs won the GPU era.
Variance reduction without its hypothesis. Quiz 2. Antithetic wants monotonicity, control variates want correlation, Sobol wants smoothness+low effective dimension, importance sampling wants bounded weights. Check, then measure, then trust.
Fitting rates to single runs. The measured −0.80. Trials, RMS, bars — even for measuring the method itself.
fp32 tallies of many small contributions. 1e8 paths × fp32 accumulator = A6's absorption eating your rare-event tally exactly (rare events are small numbers added to big sums — the worst case). fp64 or Kahan tallies (B8's ladder), always for the ACCUMULATORS.
Divergent payoff branches at warp scale. Barrier/absorption logic splits warps (5.6): profile branch efficiency; sort paths by expected fate or regenerate compacted batches (B9's compaction) when the tax shows. Randomness in VALUES needn't mean randomness in CONTROL FLOW.

Scenario — the pricing desk that bought algebra instead of a cluster

A mid-size trading firm's exotic-options desk runs nightly risk on a 40-GPU farm: 2.1 hours, and the business wants sub-hour plus a new product line that would triple the load. The infrastructure quote for the naive fix — ~120 more GPUs — lands on a quant's desk for review, and the quant, B-track-literate, asks for one week to audit the ESTIMATORS instead. Findings, in the lesson's exact vocabulary: the Asian-option pricers ran plain MC where the payoff is monotone in the driving Brownian increments (antithetic: measured 11× on their actual books — not 62×, THEIR integrand, THEIR measurement); the barrier products' rare-knockout tallies used no importance sampling (a tilted drift + reweighting: 40× on the tails that dominated the error budget); the Greeks used finite-difference re-simulation with INDEPENDENT streams (common random numbers — same seeds across the bumped runs — cut Greek variance 25× for free: correlation as a feature); and one legacy pricer carried quiz 1's cousin, a seed scheme that wrapped at 256 workers — its error bars had been fictional for two years (backtest deviations everyone had blamed on "market regime"). Net after one month of algebra: nightly risk in 41 minutes on the EXISTING farm with calibrated bars, new product line absorbed, purchase order cancelled — approximately one engineer-year of GPUs replaced by four theorems with checked hypotheses. The desk's new review rule, straight from this lesson: every estimator PR states its variance, its reduction techniques WITH their hypotheses, and its calibration evidence — because in Monte Carlo, the cheapest hardware is a smaller σ².

Practice

1. Compute required N for: (a) ±0.01 on a probability near 0.5; (b) ±1e-5 on the same; (c) ±5% RELATIVE on a rare event with p ≈ 1e-6 (plain MC); (d) case (c) with importance sampling that boosts the event to p̃ ≈ 0.1 (bounded weights). State the lesson each number teaches.
σ² for Bernoulli = p(1−p). (a) (1.96·0.5/0.01)² ≈ 9.6e3 — cheap. (b) ±1e-5: ≈ 9.6e9 — the √-law's brutality: 1000× tighter = 1e6× more samples. (c) relative bar: need σ/(√N·p) = 0.05/1.96 → N ≈ (1.96)²·(1−p)/(0.05²·p) ≈ 1.5e9 samples to SEE ~1500 events — rare events cost 1/p (the transport/finance tail problem in one line). (d) with the event at 0.1 and controlled weights: N ≈ (1.96²·9)/(0.0025·…) ~ 1.4e4-class — importance sampling bought ~1e5× (quiz 2's redirect, priced). Lessons: the square law forgives nothing (b), rare events are 1/p-expensive raw (c), and measure-change algebra is the only civilized answer (d) — with ESS monitoring as its seatbelt (§2).
2. Prove (two lines) why antithetic pairing can never INCREASE the cost for monotone f, and construct the explicit f where it doubles the variance — then state the general condition (sign of Cov[f(U), f(1−U)]) and how you'd CHEAPLY estimate it before committing.
Var of the pair-mean = ¼[2σ² + 2Cov(f(U), f(1−U))] per two samples vs σ²/2 plain: antithetic helps iff Cov < 0. Monotone f: U and 1−U are antithetic in rank → f values anti-correlate → Cov ≤ 0 (Chebyshev's other inequality — equality only for constant f): never worse, per-sample-pair. Adversarial f: symmetric about ½, e.g. f = |U−½| → f(U) = f(1−U) exactly → Cov = +σ²: pair variance DOUBLES per sample (you sampled everything twice). Cheap pre-check: 1e4 pilot samples, compute corr(f(U), f(1−U)) directly — one line, and it converts quiz 2's "sometimes 0.95×" mystery into a printed number before any commitment. Hypothesis-checking as a pilot run: the lesson's doctrine made mechanical.
3. Your GPU kernel draws exponentials via −ln(u)/λ. Enumerate the three float dangers in that one line (u=0, u near 1, fp32 log precision), their physical symptoms in a transport code, and the standard hardenings.
(1) u = 0: ln(0) = −inf → infinite free path: photon exits the universe; cuRAND uniforms exclude 0 by convention (know your generator's interval — (0,1] vs [0,1): read the doc, don't assume) or use −ln(1−u) with u∈[0,1). (2) u→1⁻: −ln(1−u)-form loses precision as 1−u cancels (M1 L8) → quantized SHORT paths: subtle spatial banding near sources (log1p is the fix: −log1p(−u)). (3) fp32 log's ~1e-7 relative error is FINE for path lengths but its argument's granularity means the smallest representable paths quantize: harmless in most tallies, visible in ultra-thin-shell geometries — fp64 the geometry-critical draws only (A6's per-tensor-role logic, per-VARIABLE here). Symptoms-first thinking: each numerics flaw maps to a physical artifact a domain scientist will describe to you — translating their "weird banding near the source" into "log1p missing" is the collaboration superpower this track builds.
4. Common random numbers (the scenario's Greek trick): explain why using the SAME streams for f(θ) and f(θ+dθ) slashes the variance of the finite-difference derivative, the condition under which it fails, and the cuRAND feature that makes "same streams" trivial to guarantee.
Var[f̂(θ+dθ) − f̂(θ)] = Var+Var − 2Cov: independent streams → 2σ², and dividing by tiny dθ amplifies it catastrophically (B1's quiz-2 V, stochastic edition — FD of noisy functions is noise division). Same streams → the paths are nearly identical, Cov ≈ σ² → the difference isolates the θ-effect: variance drops by orders (measured 25× at the desk). Fails when the payoff's PATH STRUCTURE changes discontinuously with θ (a barrier crossing flips for some paths → the coupling breaks exactly on the paths that matter — smoothing/conditional expectations are the field's patch). The cuRAND guarantee: counter-based Philox — streams are (seed, subsequence, offset) FUNCTIONS, so "same streams" = same three integers, independent of scheduling, thread count, or run (b4-streams' theorem earning its third job: independence ACROSS workers, reproducibility ACROSS runs, coupling ACROSS scenarios).
5. Design the divergence remedy for a barrier-option kernel where ~7% of paths knock out early each step (5.6's tax measured at 1.9× on the naive version): compare per-thread early-exit, warp-level compaction every k steps, and regenerate-fresh-paths-in-dead-lanes — with the bookkeeping cost and statistical caveat of each.
Early-exit: dead threads idle in the warp (one-sided if — 5.6 P2d: cheap divergence but idle lanes accumulate: at 7%/step, half the warp is dead by step ~10 → ~2× waste on long horizons: matches the measured 1.9×). Compaction every k steps (B9's stream compaction): pack live paths densely → full warps resume; cost: a scan+scatter every k steps (price via 5.8/B9's primitives — worth it when idle% × steps-remaining exceeds compaction's ~2-pass cost; k tunes it). Regenerate-in-dead-lanes: fill dead lanes with FRESH paths → zero idleness, but the tally bookkeeping must weight cohorts correctly (paths no longer share step-counts — per-path accumulators, slightly fatter state) and the caveat: fresh paths must use fresh SUBSEQUENCES (quiz 1's law) or you've correlated your replacement population. Production transport codes do compaction+regeneration ("population control" — Russian roulette/splitting are its statistical cousins from quiz 2). The meta: divergence remedies are 5.6 mechanics + STATISTICAL bookkeeping — both ledgers must balance, which is exactly why MC engineering is its own craft.
6. Write the "estimator review checklist" the scenario's desk adopted — six lines, each with its mechanism citation and the incident class it pre-empts (harvest this lesson + B8 previews).
(1) Stated σ² and required-N arithmetic vs the error budget (worked ex) — pre-empts fleet purchases that algebra could cancel.
