What it teaches

The memory system moves data in 32-byte sectors, and coalescing is judged across the 32 lanes at ONE instant (5.7's instant rule). Perfectly consecutive floats fill 4 sectors with zero waste. A stride, an array-of-structs field, or a scatter spreads the lanes across many sectors, so the hardware fetches bytes it throws away — that's the tax. The floor for fp32 is 8× (a 32-byte sector holds 8 floats, so one float per sector wastes 7/8); for fp64 it's 4× (4 doubles per sector). Switch the dtype and the pattern and watch the floor change — this is exactly the executed stride cliff from 5.7 (measured floor ~1/8 of stride-1 bandwidth).

Try: fp32 stride-1 (tax 1×, coalesced) → stride-2 (2×) → stride-8 or AoS (8×, the floor). Then switch to fp64 and see the floor drop to 4×. The gather is data-dependent — its tax depends on where the indices land (embeddings live here).
