What it teaches

Every format splits its bits into sign, exponent (dynamic range — how big/small), and mantissa (precision — how many significant digits). This is the whole story of mixed precision (A6): bf16 keeps fp32's 8 exponent bits (same range, so training doesn't overflow) but drops mantissa to 7 bits (less precision) — which is why it's the training default. fp16 has more mantissa (10 bits) but only 5 exponent bits, so it overflows around 65504 and needs loss scaling. fp8 comes in two flavors — e4m3 (more precision, saturates at 448) and e5m2 (more range, has infinity) — the Hopper/Blackwell trade. fp4 (Blackwell) has almost nothing: try 0.3 and watch it snap to 0.5. Watch the absolute error grow as you drop down the formats — that's the price of every byte you save on the bus (4.6).
