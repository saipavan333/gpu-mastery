How to read it

The diagonal is the memory roof — performance you can reach is limited to (arithmetic intensity × bandwidth), because you can only compute as fast as you can feed the ALUs. The flat line is the compute roof — the chip's peak FLOP/s. They meet at the ridge point (intensity = peak_compute / peak_bandwidth): kernels to its left are memory-bound (add intensity — fuse, cache, reuse), kernels to its right are compute-bound (you're near the FLOP ceiling; only faster math or a bigger chip helps). If you enter an achieved number below the roof, the gap between it and the ceiling is exactly the performance you haven't captured yet — the to-do list (4.7).

Landmarks from the core: on Colab's T4 (8.1 TF, ~220 GB/s achievable) the ridge sits at ~37 FLOP/byte, so SAXPY (~0.08) and reductions (~0.25) are deeply memory-bound, while a well-tiled matmul climbs toward the compute roof — exactly the 5.11 ladder.
