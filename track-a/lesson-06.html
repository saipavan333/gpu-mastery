Mixed Precision & Tensor Cores — buying speed with bits, without buying regret

Halving your dtype halves every byte on 4.6's ledger and unlocks the matrix units that do most of a modern GPU's work. The price is paid in M1 L8's currency — range and precision — and this lesson makes every line of that price list MEASURED: where fp16 flushes to zero, where sums silently stall at 4% of their true value, and which shapes the tensor cores refuse to eat.

Concept

The format menu: range vs precision, on one axis

format   exp/mant   eps (rel step)   max        the one-line character
fp32      8 / 23     1.2e-7          3.4e38     the reference
tf32      8 / 10     ~1e-3           3.4e38     fp32 range, TC speed (Ampere+
                                                 default INSIDE matmuls only)
bf16      8 / 7      7.8e-3          3.4e38     fp32's range, 2 decimal digits —
                                                 training's favorite
fp16      5 / 10     9.8e-4          65504      3 digits, TINY range: flush
                                                 below 2⁻²⁴≈6e-8, inf above 65504
fp8 e4m3  4 / 3      ~0.06           448        weights/activations (Hopper+)
fp8 e5m2  5 / 2      ~0.13           57344      gradients (range over precision)
# all measured/spec — memorize eps and max for bf16 and fp16; they explain
# 90% of mixed-precision incidents you'll ever debug.

The two failure modes, measured

RANGE failure (fp16's specialty):  grads below 2⁻²⁴ ≈ 6e-8 flush to ZERO
  (executed: fp16(2e-8) = 0.0; fp16(1e-6) survives as a subnormal) — small
  gradients simply cease to exist → dead learning signal.
PRECISION failure (everyone's):  absorption — S + g rounds to S once
  g < S·eps/2 (M1 L8's law). Executed: summing 65,536 grads of 1e-4 in
  pure fp16 STALLS at 0.25 vs true 6.55 — 96% of the mass silently lost,
  stall point exactly where eps predicts (1e-4 ÷ (eps/2) ≈ 0.2).
# The two failures have two different antidotes — scaling for range,
# wide accumulation for precision — and confusing them is the classic
# debugging dead-end (quiz 1).

The mixed-precision recipe (what torch.amp actually does)

storage/matmuls in half precision (fp16 or bf16)  ← speed + memory
master weights + optimizer states in fp32          ← updates ~1e-6·w must not
                                                     absorb into w (the §2 law!)
accumulations in fp32 (matmul acc, reductions)     ← A3 quiz 2 / 5.11's law
fp16 only: LOSS SCALING — multiply loss by S (~2¹⁶) → grads shift above the
  flush line → unscale before optimizer → if any inf/nan: skip step, S /= 2;
  every ~2k clean steps: S ×= 2 (dynamic scaling = an autotuner for range)
bf16: same recipe MINUS the scaler — 8 exponent bits mean nothing flushes;
  you pay in eps (7.8e-3): bf16 needs the fp32-acc discipline even harder.

Tensor cores: the units your dtypes unlock

what runs on TCs: matmuls/convs with fp16/bf16/tf32/fp8 inputs, dims
  friendly to the unit's tiles (multiples of 8/16; pad 1000→1024 and WIN —
  5.7 P2's pitch economics at model scale)
what doesn't: fp32-in/fp32-acc GEMMs on pre-Ampere (T4: fp16 TCs only —
  your Colab card accelerates fp16 matmuls ~4×, nothing else), elementwise
  anything (TCs are matmul furniture; your A5 fused kernels run on the
  regular ALUs regardless of dtype)
accumulate: ALWAYS fp32 in the MMA (the hardware offers it; tl.dot's
  acc dtype and cuBLAS's compute type are where you SAY it — 5.11's law
  has been hardware-assisted all along).

Worked example

Task: The precision audit of one training step of a 1B-param model in fp16-with-scaler vs bf16 — every row measured or spec-derived, the decision defensible to a review. (The measured rows come from this lesson's executed experiments; reproduce them in Ex.1.)

concern                     fp16 + dynamic scaling         bf16, no scaler
weights/activations         2 B/param ✓ same               2 B/param ✓ same
grad flush (range)          RESCUED by S≈2¹⁶ (grads        never flushes (fp32
                            6e-8→scaled 4e-3 > 2⁻²⁴ ✓)      range) ✓
optimizer update absorb     fp32 master weights ✓           fp32 master weights ✓
  (lr·g ≈ 1e-7·w vs eps)    (in fp16 masters: update <      (bf16 masters would
                             w·eps/2=4.9e-4·w → VANISHES)    lose at 3.9e-3·w!)
long reductions             fp32 acc ✓ (pure-fp16 acc       fp32 acc MANDATORY
                            measured: 96% mass lost)        (bf16 eps 8× worse)
step-skip machinery         needed (inf detection + S       none — simpler loop,
                            backoff: occasional dead steps)  no skipped steps
verdict: bf16 where hardware has it (Ampere+) — simpler and unflushable;
fp16+scaler on T4-class (your Colab!) — and EITHER way the fp32 spine
(masters + accumulations) is non-negotiable: it's where the correctness lives.

The one number to internalize: an optimizer update is typically lr·g ≈ 1e-6…1e-8 of the weight's magnitude, while half-precision eps/2 is 5e-4 (fp16) or 4e-3 (bf16). Updates are 100–10,000× SMALLER than the absorption threshold — training in pure half precision doesn't "lose a little accuracy," it silently applies ZERO update to most weights (§2's stalled sum, per-parameter). The fp32 master copy isn't conservatism; it's the difference between training and an expensive random-noise generator.

Key points

  • Exponent bits buy range, mantissa bits buy precision: bf16 = fp32's range with 2 digits; fp16 = 3 digits with a 6e-8…65504 window — memorize both eps values (7.8e-3, 9.8e-4).
  • Two distinct failure modes: FLUSH (fp16 grads below 2⁻²⁴ become zero — fixed by loss scaling) and ABSORPTION (S+g rounds to S at g<S·eps/2 — fixed by fp32 accumulation); measured: 96% of a 65k-grad sum lost to pure-fp16 accumulation.
  • The recipe: half-precision storage/matmuls, fp32 master weights + optimizer states + accumulations, dynamic loss scaling for fp16 only — every line exists because of a measured failure, not superstition.
  • Optimizer updates (≈1e-6·w) sit far below half-precision absorption thresholds (5e-4·w / 4e-3·w) — fp32 masters are what make training actually update weights.
  • Tensor cores eat half-precision TILES with fp32 accumulate: feed them aligned dims (pad to ×8/16 — a net win), know your hardware's menu (T4: fp16 only; Ampere+: +bf16/tf32; Hopper+: +fp8 with per-tensor scaling).
  • bf16 simplifies (no scaler, no skipped steps) but sharpens the accumulation discipline — its eps is 8× coarser than fp16's; the fp32 spine matters MORE, not less.

Quiz

1. A team moves training from fp32 to fp16+amp. Loss curves match for 3k steps, then their run plateaus visibly above the fp32 baseline. GradScaler logs show a healthy scale (~2¹⁵, occasional backoffs). A teammate says "loss scaling is failing — raise the scale cap." Diagnose with the two-failure-mode framework and name the two most likely true culprits.
Diagnosis-by-failure-mode is the lesson's whole point: flush symptoms (early divergence, zero grads, inf storms) vs absorption symptoms (quiet plateaus, stalled small-weight learning) are as different as 5.4's sticky vs non-sticky errors — and each has its own instrument (grad-zero-fraction histograms for flush; an fp32-shadow comparison run for absorption). Option 3 is refuted by a decade of fp16-trained models; option 4 is the eternal scapegoat (A2 Ex.2's oscillation lesson: exhaust the mechanism hypotheses before touching hyperparameters).
2. Same model, two runs on an A100: hidden size 4096 → 51 ms/step; a researcher's "tiny tweak" to hidden 4100 ("only 0.1% more parameters!") → 68 ms/step. Explain the 33% cliff, the general law, and the fix that costs nothing.
The staircase model replaces the linear-cost intuition everyone imports from complexity class: hardware charges per TILE, and a tile is charged whether full or ragged (5.2's tail threads, at matmul scale). torch exposes the check (dims % 8) and profilers show the kernel-name change (a2-dispatch: the slow row got dispatched). The "suspiciously round numbers" observation turns this from trivia into reading skill: model configs are full of frozen kernel-economics decisions — now you can see them.

Exercises

Exercise 1 — Reproduce this lesson's two measured failures in NumPy (runs anywhere, no GPU): (a) the flush table — np.float16 of {1e-6, 1e-7, 6e-8, 3e-8, 2e-8} with the 2⁻²⁴ line explained; (b) the absorption stall — sum 65,536 × 1e-4 in pure fp16 (expect ~0.25 vs 6.55) and find the stall point's dependence on the increment size (try 1e-3, 1e-5); then (c) fix (b) three ways and rank them: fp32 accumulator, pairwise/tree summation in fp16, Kahan compensation in fp16.
(a) 1e-6 → 1.01e-6 (subnormal — representable but only ~4 significant bits!),
    2e-8 → 0.0 (below 2⁻²⁵ rounds to zero). Flush is a LINE, blur included.
(b) stalls at S ≈ increment/(eps/2): 1e-4→~0.2; 1e-3→~2.0; 1e-5→~0.02 —
    the stall point SCALES with increment: absorption is relative (M1 L8).
(c) fp32 acc: exact-ish (6.5547) — cost: one register wider — WINNER, always
    available in kernels (acc_type, A3 §1). pairwise fp16: ~6.55 ± last-bits —
    error grows O(log n) not O(n) (5.8 P7's tree argument) — good when the
    accumulator must stay half (rare). Kahan fp16: recovers most digits at
    2–4× the adds — the B8 preview; overkill here, vital in fp64-poor science.

Ranking rationale is the deliverable: fp32-acc wins on cost/benefit because the hardware GIVES you the wide accumulator (TCs accumulate fp32 natively — §TC); the exotic fixes matter only where that's not true. You have now personally generated every number in §2 — which is the difference between citing a rule and owning it.

Exercise 2 — Colab (T4, fp16 amp): train a small MLP on synthetic data twice — with torch.amp (autocast + GradScaler) and in pure fp16 (model.half(), no scaler, fp16 optimizer states — deliberately wrong). Log: scale evolution, fraction of exactly-zero grads per step, and final loss. Then ONE more run: pure fp16 but with lr artificially ×1000. Explain all three outcomes with §2's two failure modes.
expected: amp run — scale climbs 2¹⁶±, zero-grad fraction ~0%, loss matches
fp32 reference; pure-fp16 — zero-grad fraction grows (small grads flush) AND
loss plateaus early (updates absorb into fp16 weights: BOTH failure modes,
compounding — the worked example's "noise generator"); pure-fp16 + lr×1000 —
updates now EXCEED the absorption threshold so weights DO move (plateau
later/lower!) but training is unstable/lossy: you "fixed" absorption by
breaking optimization — a diagnostic contrast, not a remedy.

The lr×1000 run is the teaching move: it experimentally SEPARATES the two failure modes (it cures absorption while flush remains → whatever improvement you see quantifies absorption's share). Design-of-experiments on numerics — M1 L7's controlled-variable ritual aimed inward. Log the zero-grad histogram; it's the production monitoring metric for flush (quiz 1's instrument), and you've now built it once by hand.

Go deeper — the precision engineer's fine print

1. fp8 and the scaling-factor era (Hopper/Blackwell)

fp8's 3 (e4m3) or 2 (e5m2) mantissa bits can't span a tensor's dynamic range
alone → every fp8 tensor travels WITH a scale: value ≈ fp8_code × scale,
scales chosen per-tensor (or per-block on Blackwell's nvfp4/mx formats —
finer scales, tighter fit). Training recipes keep amax histories to set
scales predictively (a rolling autotuner for range — a6-scale's idea,
per-tensor). The recipe's spine is UNCHANGED: fp32 accumulate in the MMA,
fp32 masters. fp8 halves bf16's traffic again — 4.6's ledger keeps paying —
at the cost of scale-management machinery (Transformer Engine et al. exist
to hide exactly that bookkeeping).

2. Stochastic rounding (why fp8-era optimizers survive)

round-to-nearest ALWAYS drops an update < ulp/2 → absorption (§2).
stochastic rounding: round UP with probability (update/ulp) → E[applied] =
update — individual steps are noisy, the EXPECTATION is exact: absorption
becomes unbiased noise instead of systematic zero (a Monte Carlo fix — B4's
philosophy smuggled into arithmetic). Hardware support arrives with the
fp8 optimizers; know the term — it's the answer to "how can 8-bit Adam
possibly work?"

3. Gotchas

model.half() as "mixed precision." That's PURE half — masters, states, accumulations all degraded (Ex.2's broken run). Mixed precision is a RECIPE (autocast regions + fp32 spine), not a cast.
Scaler state not checkpointed. Resume without the GradScaler's state → scale restarts at default → a burst of inf-skipped or flushed steps distorts the curve right after every resume. scaler.state_dict() belongs in the checkpoint (tiny, forgotten weekly).
Judging fp16 by early loss. Flush/absorption bite the SMALL gradients — early training (big grads) looks identical; divergence appears mid-run (quiz 1's 3k steps). Precision A/Bs need full-length runs or shadow-fp32 comparisons, not smoke tests.
bf16 checkpoint round-trips. Saving fp32 masters through a bf16-casting serialization path quantizes them (eps 7.8e-3!) — resumed training limps mysteriously. Assert checkpoint dtypes in the save/load fixture (M2 L6 for files).
Softmax/norms autocast in half. autocast keeps risky ops (softmax, norms, exp) in fp32 by POLICY — hand-written kernels bypass that policy: your A5 fused ops must re-implement it (fp32 acc + shifts) or quietly reintroduce the failures autocast was hiding (A3 quiz 2's whole arc).
Padding shame. Refusing to pad 4100→4112 "wastes 0.3% memory" while eating quiz 2's 33% staircase penalty. Padding is not waste; it's the cheapest performance purchase in the catalog (5.7 P2, a6-tc, one more time with feeling).

Scenario — the 4 a.m. inf storm and the two-knob confusion

A training run on 64 GPUs (fp16 + dynamic scaling, because the fleet still has V100s) dies at 4 a.m., step 71,204: loss = inf, then NaN cascade. The on-call's first instinct — "lower the learning rate" — is the eternal wrong first move (quiz 1's scapegoat). A course-literate engineer walks the failure-mode tree instead: inf appeared FIRST in the loss (not grads) → forward-pass range problem → which op can exceed 65504 in fp16? The attention logits: this run had just crossed into a longer-sequence curriculum phase at step 71k (config archaeology), and with ALiBi biases at the new length, pre-softmax scores pushed past fp16's ceiling BEFORE the max-shift could save them (A5 P3's flagged interaction, live: the shift happens after S is materialized in fp16). The fix menu, ranked: compute S in fp32 inside the fused kernel (the autocast policy, restored — one line in the Triton dot's acc dtype), or scale Q by 1/√d earlier (algebraically identical, range-cheaper — M1 L8 thinking), or clamp logits (a hack that changes the model; rejected). One-line fix, run resumed from checkpoint, and the postmortem's title is the lesson: "Loss scaling manages GRADIENT range; it cannot save a FORWARD pass that overflows — know which knob owns which failure." The monitoring add: an amax histogram on attention scores (the fp8-era tool from §1, deployed early), which would have shown the ceiling approach 10k steps in advance.

Practice

1. For each tensor in an amp training step, name its correct dtype and the failure if you get it wrong: weights (compute copy), master weights, activations, attention logits, gradients in flight, Adam's m and v, the loss scalar.
Compute weights: bf16/fp16 (speed; wrong→slow only). Masters: fp32 (else updates absorb — worked ex). Activations: half (memory; risky ops' internals fp32 by policy). Attention logits: fp32 acc or scaled early (scenario's inf storm). In-flight grads: half + scaled (fp16) — flush if unscaled. Adam m: half-able with care; v: fp32 or carefully-scaled — v is a sum of SQUARES of small numbers: squares of 1e-4 are 1e-8, right at flush territory, and v feeds a sqrt+divide (error amplifier). Loss: fp32 (it's one number; scaling machinery reads it). The v-answer is the interview separator: precision policy is per-TENSOR-ROLE, driven by each tensor's value distribution — not per-model.
2. Derive the loss-scale window for a model whose smallest useful grads are ~1e-7 and largest are ~10: which scales S keep everything alive in fp16, and what does the window's WIDTH tell you about when dynamic scaling starts skipping steps?
Lower bound: 1e-7·S > 2⁻²⁴ ≈ 6e-8 → S > 0.6 — trivially met. Real lower bound is subnormal-precision, not flush: want 1e-7·S above the NORMAL threshold 6.1e-5 for full mantissa → S > 610. Upper: 10·S < 65504 → S < 6550. Window ≈ [2¹⁰, 2¹²] — narrow! Dynamic scaling probes upward ×2 until inf (backoff): with a 3-octave window it hovers near the top, and any gradient-spike event (batch outlier) overflows → skip+halve → re-climb: the scale TRAJECTORY is a seismograph of your gradient distribution's tails. A wide-tailed model (unstable) skips often — scale logs are a training-health metric, not plumbing noise (quiz 1 read them exactly this way).
3. tf32 gives matmuls a 10-bit mantissa silently on Ampere+ (torch's default for fp32 GEMMs). Construct the case FOR leaving it on and the case for torch.backends.cuda.matmul.allow_tf32=False, and name the two workload classes where the answer flips.
For ON: ~3–8× matmul speed at eps 1e-3 per-op — deep nets are noise-tolerant (gradient noise ≫ 1e-3 rounding), a decade of models trained fine. For OFF: per-op relative error 1e-3 compounds in ill-conditioned chains; anything doing LINEAR ALGEBRA as the product (B-track solvers, computing physical quantities, gradcheck baselines!, unit-test oracles) needs true fp32. Flips: (1) scientific/HPC workloads embedded in torch (off — B8's reproducibility world); (2) fixture/oracle computation — your A3 reference-vs-kernel tests must pin the flag or "failures" appear when the REFERENCE silently ran tf32 (a real CI heisenbug class: the fixture changed, not the kernel). Meta-lesson: defaults encode a workload assumption; know when you're not the assumed workload.
4. Your fp8 (e4m3) linear layer shows amax = 380 on activations (format max 448). The scale-setter proposes headroom via scale ÷2 "to be safe." Price the proposal in mantissa terms and give the correct policy (delayed scaling), with the failure it actually protects against.
e4m3 has 3 mantissa bits: values live on a grid of ~6% relative spacing. Halving the scale halves every represented magnitude's precision usage — you spend ONE of your ~3 effective bits on headroom (values now top out at 190/448: the top octave unused = wasted code space; quantization RMS roughly doubles). Correct policy: scales from a rolling amax HISTORY (delayed scaling — set next step's scale from recent maxima + small margin), so codes fill the range; the failure it guards is an amax SPIKE overflowing to inf mid-step (then: per-tensor skip or clamp-with-flag, logged like scaler backoffs). fp8 engineering is 90% this bookkeeping — §1's Transformer-Engine reason — and the octave-costs-a-bit arithmetic is the fastest way to sound fluent in it.
5. Quantify quiz 2's staircase for a (4096×4100)·(4100×4096) bf16 GEMM on TC hardware: how much of the work runs on the ragged path vs padded (4096×4112), and why does the measured penalty (~30%) exceed the naive ragged-fraction estimate? (Two mechanisms.)
Ragged fraction naive estimate: only the K-dim's last 4 columns of 4100 (4100 = 256·16 + 4) → 4/4100 ≈ 0.1% of MACs — yet the penalty is ~30%: the fraction model is wrong because (1) the FALLBACK is kernel-wide, not edge-wide: an unfriendly K often demotes the ENTIRE GEMM to a different kernel (dispatcher picks per-shape, not per-tile — a2-dispatch), and (2) alignment: 4100·2B = 8200B rows misalign every row start (5.7 P2's sector tax on ALL loads, not just edge tiles). Padding to 4112 restores both: TC kernel + aligned rows, at 0.4% extra memory. The general audit: when a small shape change causes a big time change, diff the PROFILER'S KERNEL NAMES first (5.10) — the staircase is usually a dispatch cliff, not gradual raggedness.
6. Design the "precision regression" CI for a training codebase (A3 §CI's sibling): the three cheap per-commit checks and the one expensive weekly run that together catch quiz 1's plateau, the scenario's inf storm, and gotcha 4's checkpoint quantization BEFORE they reach a real run.
Per-commit: (1) dtype-audit walk — assert every parameter/optimizer-state/buffer dtype against a declared manifest (catches stray .half()/bf16 checkpoint paths — gotcha 4, quiz 1 culprit 2); (2) acc-dtype grep+test on custom kernels — the A3 fixture asserting fp32 accumulation via a mean-1e3 adversarial row (A5 Ex.1's family); (3) amax smoke test — one forward at max configured sequence length asserting attention scores fit the compute dtype's range with 2× margin (the scenario, pre-caught at PR time). Weekly: a 2k-step shadow run — half-precision config vs fp32 reference on fixed data/seed, asserting loss-curve divergence under a budget and zero-grad-fraction under a threshold (the only test that sees ABSORPTION-class drift — quiz 1's instrument, scheduled). Cost: minutes + one GPU-hour weekly; the incidents it pre-empts each cost engineer-weeks (every one of them appears in this lesson because it happened to someone at 4 a.m.).
7. Interview closer: "Why does mixed-precision training work, and what breaks when it doesn't?" — 90 seconds: the two-failure-mode framework with one measured number each, the recipe's four lines and which failure each line answers, the bf16-vs-fp16 decision, the TC shape law, and the closing sentence about where correctness actually lives.
Skeleton: "Half precision halves memory traffic and unlocks tensor cores — but floats fail two distinct ways, and the recipe answers both. Range: fp16 flushes below 2⁻²⁴ — I've measured 2e-8 becoming exactly zero — so small gradients vanish; loss scaling shifts them into the live window, dynamically probing the ceiling [a6-scale]. Precision: absorption — adding a small number to a big accumulator rounds to nothing; I've measured a 65k-element fp16 sum stalling at 4% of its true value — so accumulations and master weights stay fp32: an optimizer update is ~1e-6 of a weight, a hundred times below half-precision's rounding step, so without fp32 masters most updates apply as zero [worked ex]. bf16 trades mantissa for fp32's exponent range — no flushing, no scaler, simpler loop — at 8× coarser eps, which makes the fp32 spine MORE critical, not less. Tensor cores want half-precision tiles at multiple-of-8/16 dims — matmul cost is a staircase, so pad 4100 to 4112 and win 30% [quiz 2]. When mixed precision 'breaks,' I diagnose by failure mode — inf storms are range (forward or unscaled grads), quiet plateaus are absorption (an fp16 accumulator hiding somewhere) — because each has different knobs, and turning the wrong one is a week of 4 a.m. [scenario]. The one-liner: speed lives in the half-precision bytes; correctness lives in the fp32 spine — mixed precision is the discipline of never confusing the two."
