Occupancy Calculator

Occupancy is how many warps are resident per SM versus the max — the dial from 5.6. It's set by three budgets competing for the SM: registers, shared memory, and thread/block slots. Turn each knob and watch which resource binds and what occupancy you actually get.

What it teaches

Each SM has a fixed budget of registers, shared memory, and warp/block slots. A block consumes some of each; the SM fits as many blocks as the tightest budget allows, and that's your occupancy. High register usage (a complex kernel) or a big shared-memory tile can throttle occupancy — the classic 5.6 trade. But occupancy is a means, not an end: past "enough" warps to hide memory latency (Little's law), more occupancy doesn't help, and sometimes fewer-but-fatter threads (more registers, ILP, float4) beat more warps. Use this to find the limiting resource — then decide whether raising occupancy is even the right move (4.7's roofline tells you if you're latency-starved or already bound).

Try it: T4 preset, 256 threads, 0 smem. Step registers/thread 32 → 64 → 96 and watch occupancy fall from 100% to 50% as registers become the binding budget. That's the register-pressure story from 5.6, quantified.
